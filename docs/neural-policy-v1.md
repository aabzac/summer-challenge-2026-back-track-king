# neural-v1: an imitation policy learned from the arena leader (2026-09-20)

A new, independent strategy whose main logic is not a search and not a hand-written valuation:
two small neural networks choose the DISRUPT region and the placement cells, trained by
behaviour cloning on the 282 replays of the Legend #1 player Indrill
([indrill-study-v1.md](indrill-study-v1.md), [indrill-construction-v1.md](indrill-construction-v1.md)).
Runnable as the local bot id `neural-v1`; packaged with `gradlew submit -PsubmitVersion=neural-v1`.
Nothing in the selected v09 policy, `Params` defaults or frozen versions changes.

## 1. Which neural approach, and why

| approach | verdict for this contest |
|---|---|
| **Behaviour cloning of the leader** (supervised conditional-logit policy over engine-grounded candidates) | **chosen.** The data exists (14,423 turns, 31,594 placements, all reproduced exactly by `btk.core`); the leader is himself a sub-millisecond heuristic (0.2 ms/turn), so his rules are learnable from local features; inference is ~5 ms/turn; ~4.8k weights fit the 100k-character source limit (13.5k base64 characters). |
| AlphaZero-style self-play (MCTS + value/policy net) | rejected: simultaneous moves, ~120 legal cells x 3 placements x ~34 regions per turn, millions of self-play games needed; the local referee plays ~1,000 games in 15 minutes; a net that helps would not fit the source limit. |
| Neural evaluator inside RolloutBot | rejected: the user asked for a main logic that differs from the rollout/portfolio bots; also inherits their rollout-time limits. |
| Raw-grid CNN | rejected for v1: 14k turns are few for a spatial model; engine-derived features (exact income deltas, cheapest-route membership, bypass/take-over routes) carry the information a CNN would have to rediscover. Kept as a possible v2 input. |
| Evolutionary / policy-gradient fine-tuning of the small net with the local arena | not done in v1, but the natural follow-up: the net is tiny, `bench` is the fitness function. |

Behaviour cloning has one known failure mode, covariate shift: our own games drift into states
the leader never saw. It is mitigated by (a) computing features with the exact engine rather than
from raw grids, so a feature means the same thing in any state, (b) two hard rules for the one
situation where imitation is known to be wrong (section 3), and (c) the bench below, which is the
real test.

## 2. Architecture

All code is in `btk.bot.neural` (ships only in `neural-v1` builds):

- `NeuralFeatures` - the single definition of every feature, used by the exporter and the bot.
  Per turn: region aggregates, active-path membership, and route quantities from a feature-side
  Dijkstra (`cheapestCostPath`, same optimal cost as the referee-exact `Pathing.cheapestPath`,
  asserted by `NeuralBotTest`, but without the AutobuildAStar tie-break flood-fill, 5x faster);
  per-game memory reconstructed from the public board only (when each track appeared, who hit
  which region: an instability rise is ours if the region was our recorded target, otherwise the
  opponent's). Sabotage rows (29 features per legal region) include the exact income deltas of
  inking it now, tracks by owner and activity, the opponent's recent placements, completion-route
  traffic (shared / opponent-own-only / our-own-only), stickiness flags and game context. Build
  rows (42 features per affordable cell) include cost, region state, 4/8-neighbourhood ownership,
  membership in shared / own-only / opponent-own-only completion routes with their cost and net
  gain, bypass routes around regions at instability >= 2, take-over routes of opponent-majority
  connections, the exact income change of placing the cell alone (only computed for cells with
  two passable neighbours; otherwise it is provably zero), continuation flags and context
  (placement index, paint left, our target region).
- `NeuralNet` - ReLU MLP scorer, int16 per-layer-scaled weights plus float32 feature mean/inverse
  std, decoded from base64 at construction. Standardisation is shipped explicitly: folding it into
  the first layer spread the weights over feature scales 1e-3..1e3 and int16 quantisation then
  destroyed the small ones (holdout top-1 fell from 46% to 31% in the smoke run).
- `NeuralWeights` - generated by `tools/neural/train_neural.py`; the only file that changes when
  the model is retrained.
- `NeuralBot` - per turn: observe -> full `prepare` -> sabotage head over all legal regions ->
  record target -> up to three placements, each scored by the build head on the board that
  already holds this turn's earlier placements (incremental `prepare` after each one) -> output
  `PLACE_TRACKS ...;DISRUPT r`. Reads only `NOISE`/`SEED` (additive Gaussian logit noise to break
  self-play mirrors), `USE_CLOSE`, `USE_DISRUPT`, `DEBUG` from `Params`
  (`versions/neural-v1.properties`). Time guard: no further placement is started with less than
  6 ms left.
- `btk.tools.CgNeuralExport` (`gradlew cgNeuralExport`) - replays the corpus through the core and
  writes candidate-set datasets: every DISRUPT among the legal regions, every accepted
  PLACE_TRACKS (stdout order) among the affordable candidates of the intermediate board. Training
  games keep the chosen row plus 40 sampled negatives; the last 40 games (holdout, by game id)
  keep the full candidate set for honest top-1 numbers.
- `tools/neural/train_neural.py` - numpy only (no torch on this machine): softmax over the
  candidate rows of one decision, cross-entropy on the chosen row, Adam with cosine decay, 40
  epochs, a linear conditional-logit trained alongside as a baseline, quantisation check on the
  holdout, evidence JSON and Java export.

## 3. Hard rules kept outside the network

Indrill's 17 losses are all early closures with him behind; in 10 of them his own DISRUPT
delivered the closing ink ([indrill-study-v1.md](indrill-study-v1.md) section 5). The
imitation therefore refuses any ink that would end the game while we are not ahead, and takes an
ink that ends the game when we lead by at least `CLOSE_MARGIN` = 6 after it (simultaneous
placements can still score). Everything else is the trained weights.

## 4. Training result (2026-09-20, `docs/evidence/neural-v1-training.json`)

Corpus 282 games / 14,423 turns; holdout = the 40 latest games (1,860 sabotage and 4,201
placement decisions with full candidate sets); zero decisions without a target; 1,330 / 31,594
leader placements (4.2%) fell outside the candidate filter (far filler cells) and were skipped.

| head | candidates / decision | parameters | holdout top-1 | top-3 | uniform | linear logit baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sabotage (29 -> 32 -> 16 -> 1) | 34.6 | 1,505 | **55.5%** | 77.2% | 3.1% | 50.2% |
| build (42 -> 48 -> 24 -> 1) | 114.0 | 3,265 | **23.4%** | 42.5% | 1.1% | 20.4% |

Per placement index (build head): k=0 22.7% of 124 candidates, k=1 25.6% / 113, k=2 21.5% / 98.
Quantised int16 networks reproduce the float holdout numbers (55.5% / 23.4%). The earlier
conditional-logit fit of the Indrill study reached 38% top-1 on the same task with fewer features;
the sabotage head's gain over its own linear baseline (+5 points) says the extra value is mostly in
the features (exact ink deltas, routes, memory), the nonlinearity adds the rest. Placement is
inherently ambiguous (the order of cells within one route is arbitrary), so 23% top-1 / 42% top-3
against 114 candidates is a strong fit of *where* he builds, not a ceiling.

Cost: features 4.9 ms per whole turn in the exporter (prepare 4.1, sabotage rows 0.2, build rows
0.6), i.e. one full and about two incremental route recomputations; the strict process check of
the merged artifact (87,815 / 100,000 characters) had **0/8 problem games** vs v09 on boss maps,
first response <= 751 ms, later responses <= 30.6 ms (before the feature-side Dijkstra the same
check reached 49.1 ms, which is why it exists).

## 7. Why the imitation beats the search bots locally, in one paragraph

The rollout bots decide with an eight-turn exact simulation but a hand-written valuation; every
sabotage-side experiment on them (track value, live target, always-disrupt) failed because the
self-play mirror does not produce the arena meta, so their valuations were never exercised where
they matter. The imitation carries the meta itself: it disrupts every turn on the region the
leader would pick (55% exact agreement on held-out games) and it builds full paint on his
candidates, so the bench games look like arena games (early closures, ~73 turns) and the
opponent's projects get inked before they earn. It is also 6x cheaper per move, which leaves the
turn budget unused for a later search-on-top.

## 8. Limitations and next steps

- Pure imitation cannot exceed its teacher and inherits his blind spots except the closure rule;
  it also has no notion of the opponent's reply beyond what his replays implicitly encode.
- Candidate filter: cells must be next to a track/town, on a computed route, or within two steps
  of an own track; 4.2% of the leader's cells (late filler) are outside it.
- Only Indrill's decisions are used. Adding other Legend players is a one-line data change but
  mixes styles; a per-player embedding or a filter on won games would be the clean way.
- Natural continuations, in order: (1) fine-tune the same small net on local arena outcome
  (evolution strategies / policy gradient with `bench` as fitness, keeping the imitation as the
  prior) and, given the boss result, on the 40 pinned boss maps' *states* (the v09 boss replays
  are exact, so v09-vs-boss positions can be mixed into the training set as a domain bridge);
  (2) DAgger-style relabelling is impossible without the teacher, but training on our own *won*
  games against strong references is a cheap self-distillation; (3) a value head for the
  closure margin instead of the fixed constant; (4) a hybrid: the imitation as a move generator
  for the rollout's portfolio (its candidates instead of hand-built routes), which keeps v09's
  lookahead where the boss punishes its absence.
